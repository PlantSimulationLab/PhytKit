# LI-600 porometry

## Why the LI-600 overestimates conductance

The LI-600 porometer assumes the air in its chamber stays at one temperature
as it flows past the leaf. In fact, water evaporating from the leaf cools the
air (psychrometric cooling). The instrument ignores this, so it
**overestimates stomatal conductance**. The error grows at high conductance
and low humidity.

## The correction

`pk li600 correct` applies the correction of **Rizzo & Bailey (2025)**. For
each reading it restores the energy balance by solving three equations for
three unknowns: the outlet air temperature, transpiration E and stomatal
conductance gsw.

1. **Diffusion:** transpiration driven by the leaf-to-chamber vapour gradient,
   through the stomatal and boundary-layer conductances in series.
2. **Water balance:** transpiration from the change in water vapour between
   inlet and outlet air.
3. **Enthalpy balance:** the energy carried by the air, including heat the
   instrument adds, \(C\,(T_{in} - T_{chamber})\).

The instrument's own boundary-layer conductance (2.921 mol m⁻² s⁻¹) and
aperture area are built in. Two settings depend on your setup:

| Option | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `--sidedness` | 1 | 1 for leaves with stomata on one side, 2 for both sides. Conductance and transpiration are multiplied by it. |
| `--thermal-conductance` | 0.007 W °C⁻¹ | Heat exchange between the instrument and the chamber air |

`overestimate_pct` is \(100\,(g_{measured} - g_{corrected})/g_{corrected}\).
Readings where the solve doesn't converge are left blank.

The correction adds these columns:

- `gsw_corrected`
- `E_corrected`, in mmol m⁻² s⁻¹ like the LI-600's `E_apparent`
- the inlet, chamber and outlet temperatures (`T_in_corrected`,
  `Ta_chamb_corrected`, `T_out_corrected`)
- the chamber water mole fraction
- the sidedness used

## Estimating A from conductance

`pk li600 estimate` solves net assimilation for each porometer reading. It
uses the reading's conductance, light and leaf temperature, together with a
photosynthesis parameter set.

1. **CO₂ conductance:** \(g_{sc} = g_{sw}/1.6\).
2. **Absorbed light:** `Qamb` × absorptance (default 0.85).
3. **Solve for Ci:** find the CO₂ concentration where the photosynthesis
   model's A equals the diffusion supply, \(g_{sc}\,(C_a - C_i)\), with
   \(C_a\) = 420 µmol mol⁻¹ by default. The search starts over 0.01 to max(2000, 2·\(C_a\)) µmol mol⁻¹ and widens
   if needed, since a negative A at low conductance puts Ci above Ca.
   Readings that can't be solved are left blank, and `warnings` counts them.
4. **Mesophyll conductance:** if the parameter set has a gm between 0 and 99,
   the solve uses stomatal and mesophyll conductance in series, and reports
   both Ci and Cc. Otherwise Cc = Ci.

If the file has a `gsw_corrected` column, it's used in place of `gsw`, so
**run the correction first** for the most accurate A.

Very low or negative readings (the LI-600 reports negative gsw when the
outlet is drier than the inlet) are raised to `--min-gsw` (default 0.001) and
flagged `clamped`. With `--min-gsw 0`, they're left unsolved instead.

The parameter set can come from:

- the model's defaults,
- a `--params` file: a single `pk fit` JSON result (a batch result is rejected; pass one entry of its `results`), a CSV of fitted parameters (the
  last row is used), a two-column Parameter/Value table, or `name = value`
  lines,
- `--set name=value`.

Later sources win. Parameters in a `--params` file that the model doesn't use
are listed in `ignoredParams`; a file with none the model uses is an error, as
is an unknown name in `--set`. Without `--model`, the estimate uses the model a
`pk fit` JSON was fitted with. Giving a different `--model` than that is an
error, since the parameters belong to another model.
